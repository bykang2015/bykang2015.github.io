Boyoung (Brenda) Kang, Ph.D.

Research Professor, Department of Applied Artificial Intelligence, Sungkyunkwan University

I study how language-based AI systems behave in mental-health and other high-stakes clinical settings, and how evaluation findings can be turned into safer systems. My work combines clinical natural language processing, LLM evaluation, and human-centered study design.

I am a licensed clinical psychologist in Korea. Before moving into research, I spent about ten years in adolescent academic coaching and counseling. That experience led me to build an NLP-based youth mental-health platform as principal investigator of a Ministry of Health and Welfare project, and it still shapes the questions I ask about AI in care settings.

Deployment-realistic evaluation of clinical language models

Early-risk models for counseling dialogue are usually trained on complete sessions but must decide mid-conversation. I measure what that mismatch costs and how to train for the information a model will actually have.

Reliability of AI judgments in healthcare

LLMs are increasingly used to audit and label clinical data. I test how far their verdicts agree with each other and with experts, and when a single model's judgment can be trusted.

Conversational AI and tools for mental-health care

I design and evaluate chatbots and reflection tools for clients and counselors, from user-experience studies with Korean users to quality-assurance frameworks for therapeutic AI.
